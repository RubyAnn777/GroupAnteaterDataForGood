1 · Where

Two countries, two places, about 1,800 km apart

Both places are in the Amazon rainforest. Gold sits in river sand washed down from the Andes. Miners clear the forest, dig up the sand and wash out the gold with mercury.

Simplified outline, drawn by hand from rough coordinates. Fine for orientation, not for analysis.

Peru · main case

Madre de Dios region

Where
South-east corner of Peru, bordering Brazil and Bolivia. Capital: Puerto Maldonado.
Who mines
Thousands of small "artisanal" miners. Biggest gold-mining hotspot in the Peruvian Amazon.
Protected
Tambopata National Reserve and Bahuaja-Sonene National Park. FZS works here.
Operation
Mercurio, February 2019, at La Pampa.
Brazil · second case

Yanomami indigenous territory

Where
Far north of Brazil (states Roraima and Amazonas), bordering Venezuela. About 9.6 million ha, the largest indigenous territory in Brazil.
Who mines
Outside wildcat miners (garimpeiros) who invaded the territory.
Operation
Federal operation from February 2023, after a health emergency was declared.
2 · The rules

Can you mine here?

Mining is not illegal everywhere. It depends on where you dig. Satellites only see that forest became a mine, not whether that was legal. So we have to know which zone each place is in.

Never
Peru

Inside a protected area (national reserve, national park, communal reserve)

The core of a protected area. No mining allowed. Example: Tambopata National Reserve.

Never
Brazil

Inside an indigenous territory

The constitution (Art. 231) allows mining there only with a special law and approval by Congress. That law has never been passed, so all outside mining in indigenous territories is illegal. Example: Yanomami.

Almost never
Peru

In a buffer zone

A protective belt of land around a protected area. People live and farm there, but nothing may harm the reserve. In Madre de Dios, mining is only legal inside the mining corridor (next row), so mining in a buffer zone outside the corridor is illegal. La Pampa is in the Tambopata buffer zone, outside the corridor: illegal.

With a permit
Peru

In the mining corridor (corredor minero)

A strip of Madre de Dios set aside for small-scale mining by a 2012 law (D.L. 1100). Miners can register and become legal here. Most mining deforestation in the region happens inside this corridor (MAAP #208 puts it at about three quarters).

With a permit
Brazil

Ordinary land outside indigenous territories and protected areas

Small-scale mining (garimpo) can be legal with a mining permit from the federal agency (ANM).

To check before the brief: open D.L. 1100 (Peru) for the corridor and where it overlaps buffer zones, and cite Art. 231 §3 of the Brazilian constitution. We have only read summaries so far (MAAP #208; Brazilian court and law-firm summaries).

3 · Peru up close

A reserve, a belt around it, and a mining strip

Think of a fried egg. The yolk is the reserve (no mining). The white is the buffer zone (protective belt). Next to it runs the mining corridor along the highway, where mining can be legal.

Protected area: no mining Buffer zone: illegal outside the corridor Mining corridor: legal with a permit

Positions are illustrative. Real boundaries come from SERNANP (Peru's protected-area agency).

Why the buffer zone matters to us

Our main data table measures mining per buffer zone, not per reserve. So our "target" is the Tambopata buffer zone (where La Pampa is), and our comparison is the Amarakaeri buffer zone next door.

Why it matters to FZS

FZS supports Peru's park service in Tambopata and Bahuaja-Sonene, including patrols against mining. The FZS program area is the reserve plus about 4,500 km² of buffer zone. They want to know whether those patrols work or just push miners elsewhere.

4 · Enforcement

What "enforcement" actually was

Enforcement means police, soldiers and park rangers going in: destroying machines and camps, setting up checkpoints and staying there. It was never a single switch. It got stronger and weaker over time.

The key contrast

5 · Our study

What we are actually asking

When the police clear one mining area, what happens to the mining? There are three possible answers. In the target area alone, all three look the same at first: mining drops.

It stops

Deterrence. Miners give up. Mining falls in the target and does not rise anywhere else.

It moves

Displacement. Miners go next door: to the comparison area, the corridor or into the reserve. The total stays the same.

It pauses

Delay. Mining comes back as soon as the police leave.

How we tell them apart

  • Count new mining per year (this year minus last year), not the total stock.
  • Compare the target (Tambopata buffer zone) with a similar area that got less enforcement (Amarakaeri buffer zone), before and after 2019.
  • Add up the whole region to see if the total fell (stops) or not (moves).
  • Follow the years after the operation to see if it comes back (pauses).

6 · Treated vs control

Who got the operation, and who we compare them with

Same idea as a medical trial. The treated area got the "medicine" (the police operation). The control area is a similar place that did not, or got much less. The control shows what would have happened to the treated area without the operation.

Why we need a control at all

If we only look at Tambopata, mining fell after 2019. But maybe it would have fallen anyway: COVID, gold prices, the rains. The control area went through all of that too, just without Mercurio. So the difference between the two is our best guess of what the operation did.

Peru · Operation Mercurio
Treated

Tambopata buffer zone. Why: La Pampa, where Mercurio started in February 2019 and set up permanent police and army bases, lies inside it.

Control

Amarakaeri buffer zone. Why: same region, same gold rivers, real mining before 2019, but not the target of Mercurio.

Time

Before 2016–18 · After 2019–21 · later years (to 2025) check whether it comes back.

Weak spot: Amarakaeri was not untouched. Mining there also dropped in some places, and in 2023 it got the state of emergency too. So we compare "a lot of enforcement" with "some enforcement". And if miners moved from Tambopata into Amarakaeri, the control goes up because of the treatment, which makes the effect look bigger than it is.

Brazil · Yanomami operation
Treated

Yanomami territory. Why: the federal operation from February 2023 targeted it specifically, after the health emergency.

Control

Munduruku and Kayapó territories (in Pará). Why: the other two big gold-mining territories, with heavy mining before 2023, but not the target of the 2023 operation.

Time

Before 2020–22 · After 2023–24 (the per-territory data stop in 2024).

Weak spot: only two years after. And Munduruku and Kayapó may have had their own operations later. We have to check that before calling them a control. The 2019–22 "weak enforcement" period hit the whole country at once, so there is no control for it inside Brazil. We can only describe it.

The calculation, step by step (Peru, real numbers)

New mining per year (ha), averaged over each period. This is the "difference-in-differences": first the change in each area, then the difference between those changes.

Read it as: "compared with Amarakaeri, Tambopata added about 1,760 ha less new mining per year after Mercurio". That is a description. It only becomes a causal claim if both areas would have moved in parallel without the operation, which we have to argue (and doubt) in the brief.

Displacement check

Add up the whole region. If the total fell, mining stopped. If it stayed flat or rose, it moved. (Peru: the region rose from 3,610 to 4,640 ha/yr.)

Placebo check

Pretend the operation happened in 2016. If we still find a big "effect", our method is picking up something else.

Persistence check

Look at 2022–25. Does mining come back once enforcement weakens? (Peru: Tambopata back to about 1,800 ha/yr.)

7 · Our data

What we have, what is missing, what we add

All our mining numbers come from MapBiomas: satellite maps that label every 30 m pixel each year as forest, river, mining and so on. The course gave us a "data pack" of ready-made tables. The catch: Peru and Brazil are cut into different pieces.

Peru: counted per buffer zone

We see the Tambopata buffer zone (where La Pampa is) directly. We do not see the reserve itself, so we would miss miners who moved into it.

Brazil: per municipality, or all territories lumped

The pack has no row for Yanomami alone. Municipalities cut across the territory and also include mines outside it. The indigenous-land table adds up all territories in Brazil into one number. So for Brazil we must download the per-territory data ourselves.

Words we keep using

Mini glossary

Protected area (ANP)Land the state protects by law: national park, national reserve, communal reserve. Peru: run by SERNANP.
Buffer zone (zona de amortiguamiento)The belt around a protected area that shields it. Activities allowed only if they don't harm the reserve.
Communal reserveProtected area co-managed with indigenous communities. Amarakaeri is one.
Mining corridorThe strip in Madre de Dios where small-scale mining can be legalised (D.L. 1100, 2012).
Artisanal miningSmall-scale mining with simple machines (pumps, small dredges). Usually informal or illegal.
Garimpo / garimpeiroBrazilian word for artisanal gold mining / the miner.
Indigenous territory (Terra Indígena)Land legally reserved for indigenous peoples in Brazil. Outside mining is illegal.
La PampaThe largest illegal mining area in Peru, in the Tambopata buffer zone. Target of Mercurio.
MapBiomasSatellite-based land-use maps. Our main data: hectares of mining per area per year.
MAAPAmazon monitoring project that publishes short satellite reports (#130, #241 ...). Our main written source.
Target vs comparisonThe area that got the operation vs a similar area that did not (or got less). Comparing both before and after is a difference-in-differences.
AdditionsNew mining in a year: this year's hectares minus last year's.
Treated / controlTreated: the area that got the operation. Control: a similar area that did not (or less), showing what would have happened anyway.
Data packThe ready-made tables the course gave us in data/.
CollectionA MapBiomas release version (e.g. Collection 4). Numbers differ between collections, so never mix them.
