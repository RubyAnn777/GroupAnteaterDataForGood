# Adversarial review of report_content.md / REPORT.md (2026-10-11)

Scope: report text, numbers.csv, checks.csv, notes, peru.py, peru_robust.py, brazil.py, brief_figure.py (other modules skimmed). Line numbers refer to docs/overnight/report_content.md. Ranked by severity.

## HIGH

1. **High. Section 1, "Short answer" (l.16).** "Enforcement moved mining more than it stopped it. Mining also came back when enforcement eased." Untagged and causal in form. The data support only: the three buffer zones fell by 1,163 ha/yr (2019-21 vs 2016-18) while the MdD total rose by 1,030 ha/yr, and the MdD total rise is small against year-to-year noise (2017 = 5,033 ha, 2019-21 mean 4,640). "Came back when enforcement eased" also conflicts with your own timeline: Tambopata's 2023 spike (+3,565 ha) falls inside the state of emergency (since 7 Apr 2023), and the return starts in 2020 (COVID per MAAP #241) while Restauracion was in force in 2021.
   Fix: replace with
   `**Short answer from our data** [[D]]: Where Mercurio hit (Tambopata buffer zone), new mining fell sharply in 2019-21 and was back above its 2016-18 level in 2022-25. The nearby Amarakaeri buffer zone and the rest of Madre de Dios rose over the same years. We cannot say from this data whether that is displacement, COVID, the gold price or legal mining. [[P]] is in the box below.`

2. **High. Event-study reading (l.78) and Section 7.** "2019-22 coefficients sit far below the placebo band, then return to it from 2023. The pre-period is noisy (2016, 2017), so parallel trends are only roughly plausible." Check against output/peru_event_study.csv:
   - The 2016 pre-period coefficient is -1,195 (pool A) / -1,066 (pool B), while the placebo band that year is [+10, +389]. It is as far outside the band as the post-period ones (2019: -1,451; band [31, 332]). Pre-trend violation is the same size as the "effect". The cause is the reference year: 2018 is a high year for Tambopata (1,757 ha) and a very low one for Amarakaeri (54 ha).
   - "Return to it from 2023" is wrong for 2024 (pool A -833, B -525, band min -291) and 2025 (pool A -369, band min -272). Only 2023 is inside the band.
   Fix: `[[D]] 2019-22 coefficients are far below the placebo band, but so is the 2016 coefficient (-1,195 ha, band +10 to +389). The 2018 reference year is unusually high for Tambopata and unusually low for Amarakaeri, so the parallel-trends assumption is not supported. 2023 is inside the band; 2024-25 are below it again.` Also add to Section 7: "Pre-trend: 2016 coefficient as large as the post-period ones."

3. **High. Item 2 (l.21), placebo-in-space.** "The fall in Tambopata is unusual ... most negative ... (rank 1 of 15)." In output/peru_placebo_in_space.csv most of the 15 donors are tiny or zero-mining units (Cordillera Azul, El Sira, Huascaran, Calipuy, Lachay, Junin, Rio Abiseo ...), including non-Amazon, industrial/legal-mining zones. A zero-mining unit cannot have a large negative coefficient, so rank 1 on the raw scale is close to mechanical for the largest miner. Only Amarakaeri (rank 15, +633) is a real peer. The scaled rank covers only 11 units (NaN for zero baseline) yet the text uses n = 15 for both. Minimum possible p = 1/15 = 0.07.
   Fix: `[[D]] Tambopata has the most negative 2019-21 coefficient of 15 Peruvian buffer zones, but only 3-4 of them had meaningful mining before 2019, so this rank is nearly guaranteed by size. We read it as "no other large zone fell", not as a significance test.` Code: in peru.run_event_study restrict the donor pool to zones with mean 2014-18 additions above, say, 10 ha, and report n.

4. **High. Item 7 (l.29) and Brazil enforcement reading (l.102).** The "mirror image" uses ALL IBAMA notices (dominated by flora/deforestation). The mining-flagged series did not fall: 157 (2015-18) to 147.5 (2019-22), then 354 in 2023 (numbers.csv br_ibama_mining_aml_*). brazil_enforcement.md says explicitly "do NOT say mining-specific fines fell". Also, 2023-25 additions (2,978 ha/yr) are still more than twice 2016-18 (1,332), and the report gives no source for "weaker enforcement 2019-22" (Nunes et al. 2024 was opened and is citable). "Weaker enforcement would predict" is an interpretation.
   Fix l.29, last two sentences: `Total IBAMA notices fell with the mining rise, but notices flagged as mining (a noisy text match) did not fall ({{br_ibama_mining_aml_mean_2015_2018}} to {{br_ibama_mining_aml_mean_2019_2022}} per year). The general fall in notices is documented for deforestation enforcement by Nunes et al. (2024, Sci. Rep. 14, Fig. 1). This pattern fits weaker enforcement, but it is not evidence for it.` And l.102: `Total notices dip in 2019-22 while mining additions surge; mining-flagged notices do not dip.`
