# Literature v2: enforcement, displacement and illegal gold mining (compiled 2026-10-12)

Access date for all entries: 2026-10-12. "Opened" means I read the abstract or full text myself:
- **full**: the full text was read
- **abstract**: the abstract was read via the publisher page, OpenAlex, Europe PMC or the Semantic Scholar API
- **snippet**: seen only in search results or a secondary source; do not quote it as if we had read it

Authors, title, journal, year and DOI come from the DOI record (OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar). **Volume and page numbers were added from memory and are not verified;** check them against the DOI before the reference list.

`literature.md` (2026-10-10) already covers Mataveli et al. 2022, Lopes & Chiavari 2021, Saavedra 2022, Caballero Espejo 2018, the Hutukara/ISA report and Conley & Taber 2011. Entries here add to or update that file.

## A. Peru: Madre de Dios gold mining and enforcement

### 1. Asner, Llactayo, Tupayachi & Ráez Luna (2013), PNAS
- **Citation:** Asner GP, Llactayo W, Tupayachi R, Luna ER (2013) Elevated rates of gold mining in the Amazon revealed through high-resolution monitoring. *PNAS* 110(46):18454-18459. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1318271110 (open: PMC3832012)
- **Opened:** full (PMC HTML).
- **Key finding:** "the geographic extent of gold mining increased 400%" (1999-2012). Forest loss "tripled in 2008 [...] closely associated with increased gold prices". "Small clandestine operations now comprise more than half of all gold mining activities". Miners are "locating their operations back from the main stem of regionally important rivers [...] to better conceal their presence from law enforcement". Sediment effects "persist for hundreds of kilometers, potentially well into Brazil".
- **Use:** the gold-price channel (pre-2012); the road/river access story; a warning that satellites miss small, hidden sites. **Note:** the full text does not contain a "within 10 km of rivers" statistic.

### 2. Asner & Tupayachi (2017), Environmental Research Letters
- **Citation:** Asner GP, Tupayachi R (2017) Accelerated losses of protected forests from gold mining in the Peruvian Amazon. *Environ. Res. Lett.* 12(9):094004. https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/aa7dab
- **Opened:** full (IOP page).
- **Key finding:** "A temporary downward inflection in the annual growth rate of mining-related forest loss following 2012 government action was followed by a near doubling of the deforestation rate from mining in 2013–2014". The interdictions "are said to have eradicated 90% of miners in the buffer zone in 2012–2013", with "a fast recovery of capacity by the miners". Inside Tambopata NR, mining-related loss rose 37% after 2012.
- **Use:** the first enforcement wave (2012) and its quick reversal. This is the "delay" pattern, a precedent for 2019-21. Supports the placebo/earlier-wave test in PLAN_v2.

### 3. Swenson, Carter, Domec & Delgado (2011), PLoS ONE
- **Citation:** Swenson JJ, Carter CE, Domec J-C, Delgado CI (2011) Gold mining in the Peruvian Amazon: global prices, deforestation, and mercury imports. *PLoS ONE* 6(4):e18875. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0018875
- **Opened:** abstract (Europe PMC).
- **Key finding:** mining deforestation rose "nonlinearly alongside a constant annual rate of increase in international gold price (∼18%/yr)". Gold price is linked to Peru's mercury imports (R² = 0.93, 2003-2009). "Virtually all of Peru's mercury imports are used in artisanal gold mining."
- **Use:** the price → mining → mercury link. Part 4(b): why a gold-price confounder matters.

### 4. Caballero Espejo, Messinger, Román-Dañobeytia, Ascorra, Fernandez & Silman (2018), Remote Sensing
- **Citation:** Caballero Espejo J et al. (2018) Deforestation and forest degradation due to gold mining in the Peruvian Amazon: a 34-year perspective. *Remote Sensing* 10(12):1903. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs10121903
- **Opened:** abstract (Semantic Scholar API; the MDPI page blocks scripts).
- **Key finding:** "nearly 100,000 ha of deforestation due to ASGM" in 1984-2017; "10% of that deforestation occurred in 2017, the highest annual amount".
- **Use:** independent confirmation of the 2017 spike (§3 of the background file).

### 5. Dethier, Silman, Fernandez, Caballero Espejo, Alqahtani, Pauca & Lutz (2023), Conservation Letters
- **Citation:** Dethier EN et al. (2023) Operation Mercury: impacts of national-level armed forces intervention and anticorruption strategy on artisanal gold mining and water quality in the Peruvian Amazon. *Conservation Letters* 16(5):e12978. https://doi.org/10.1111/conl.12978
- **Opened:** abstract (Semantic Scholar) + publisher metadata (Firecrawl). Full text **not** read.
- **Key finding:** mining ponds grew "33%–90%/year" before; "after, they decreased by 4%–5%/year in targeted zones"; "70%–90% abandonment". "New mining activity accelerated in nearby areas outside the targeted area (pond area increases: 42%–83%; deforestation increases +3–5 km2/year). Far from intervention zones, mining remained stable". "some miners began to return to intervention areas when enforcement relaxed in 2022."
- **Use:** the closest published study to ours, with deterrence + displacement + delay all in one paper. Our added value: data to 2025, whole region, inside the reserve, Brazil. Check the full text for the "monthly rotation" claim before citing it.

### 6. Álvarez-Berríos, L'Roe & Naughton-Treves (2021), Environmental Research Letters
- **Citation:** Álvarez-Berríos NL, L'Roe J, Naughton-Treves L (2021) Does formalizing artisanal gold mining mitigate environmental impacts? Deforestation evidence from the Peruvian Amazon. *Environ. Res. Lett.* 16:064052. https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/abede9
- **Opened:** abstract (OpenAlex).
- **Key finding:** 2001-2014, 1,701 provisional titles and a ">5000 km2 'corridor'". "After the mining corridor was declared and enforcement increased, new mining sites were opened more frequently within titled areas and inside the corridor than elsewhere. However, mining also increased in protected area buffer zones and native communities, and the proportion of mining area occurring outside the corridor grew, concentrated in a few hotspots." Titles were used "to bolster territorial claims, avoid policing, obtain credit and recruit paying 'guest' miners who generally ignored regulations." Uses fixed effects + matching on gold price, geology and accessibility.
- **Use:** the best evidence on the 2012 corridor design. Supports the "corridor miners mine informally" hypothesis (§4b), and is a design template (matching on geology/access).

### 7. Salo et al. (2016), The Extractive Industries and Society
- **Citation:** Salo M, Hiedanpää J, Karlsson T, Cárcamo Ávila L, Kotilainen J, Jounela P (2016) Local perspectives on the formalization of artisanal and small-scale mining in the Madre de Dios gold fields, Peru. *Extr. Ind. Soc.* 3(4). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exis.2016.10.001
- **Opened:** abstract start only (OpenAlex).
- **Key finding:** questions the assumption that "clear property rights" make formalized miners follow environmental norms.
- **Use:** context for part 4 (formalization ≠ compliance). Low priority.

## B. Mercury and health (Madre de Dios)

### 8. Diringer et al. (2015), Environmental Science: Processes & Impacts
- **Citation:** Diringer SE, Feingold BJ, Ortiz EJ, Gallis JA, Araújo-Flores JM, Berky A, Pan WKY, Hsu-Kim H (2015) River transport of mercury from artisanal and small-scale gold mining and risks for dietary mercury exposure in Madre de Dios, Peru. *Environ. Sci.: Processes Impacts* 17:478-487. https://doi.org/10.1039/c4em00567h
- **Opened:** abstract (OpenAlex).
- **Key finding:** 62 sites over 560 km; Hg in sediment, suspended solids and fish raised downstream of mining; ">one-third of carnivorous fish exceeding [...] 0.5 mg kg-1"; "communities located hundreds of kilometers downstream [...] are at risk".
- **Use:** the part 4(b) "satellites can't see mercury" paragraph; how far harm travels.

### 9. Diringer et al. (2020), Environmental Science & Technology
- **Citation:** Diringer SE, Berky AJ, Marani M, Ortiz EJ, Karatum O, Plata DL, Pan WK, Hsu-Kim H (2020) Deforestation due to artisanal and small-scale gold mining exacerbates soil and mercury mobilization in Madre de Dios, Peru. *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 54(1). https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.est.9b06620
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** forest loss doubled soil mobilization in the Colorado watershed (4× in Puquiri). Mentions mercury states of emergency in 2016 and 2019.
- **Use:** links our deforestation outcome to mercury harm.

### 10. Gerson et al. (2022), Nature Communications
- **Citation:** Gerson JR, Szponar N, Zambrano AA, Bergquist B, Broadbent E, Driscoll CT, et al. (2022) Amazon forests capture high levels of atmospheric mercury pollution from artisanal gold mining. *Nat. Commun.* 13:559. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-27997-3
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** intact canopy near mining "intercepts large amounts of particulate and gaseous mercury"; Hg builds up in "soils, biomass, and resident songbirds in some of the Amazon's most protected and biodiverse areas".
- **Use:** harm inside Tambopata NR even without mining inside it. Strong for the FZS audience.

### 11. Reuben et al. (2020), GeoHealth
- **Citation:** Reuben A, Frischtak H, Berky A, Ortiz EJ, Morales AM, Hsu-Kim H, Pendergast LL, Pan WK (2020) Elevated hair mercury levels are associated with neurodevelopmental deficits in children living near artisanal and small-scale gold mining in Peru. *GeoHealth* 4:e2019GH000222. https://doi.org/10.1029/2019GH000222
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** 164 children; 32.9% above WHO 2.0 µg/g; above-reference children "scored 4.68 IQ points lower".
- **Use:** human cost; the IQ channel is what the CSF calculator values.

### 12. Martinez et al. (2018), IJERPH
- **Citation:** Martinez G, McCord SA, Driscoll CT, Todorova S, Wu S, Araújo JF, Vega CM, Fernandez LE (2018) Mercury contamination in riverine sediments and fish associated with artisanal and small-scale gold mining in Madre de Dios, Peru. *IJERPH* 15(8):1584. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph15081584
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** fish Hg was not higher in the impacted river; sediment Hg was. Fish Hg is "not an ideal bioindicator of site-specific Hg contamination".
- **Use:** honesty about measurement; a counterweight to #8.

### 13. Gasparinetti et al. (2021), CSF methodology (grey literature)
- **Citation:** Gasparinetti P, Bakker L, Queiroz J, Vilela T, Lobo F, Nagel G (2021) *Metodologia de Valoração de Impactos do Garimpo Ilegal de Ouro na Amazônia*. Conservation Strategy Fund, Série Técnica 53. https://miningcalculator.conservation-strategy.org/assets/download/metodologia.pdf
- **Opened:** full (PDF text).
- **Key finding:** Hg:Au 2.6:1 default (range 1.3-8); ~7% of Hg to water; 100 km impact radius (literature 4-100 km); 0.7-8.3 kg gold/ha; 1 kg gold = R$ 1-2 M of damage (Brazil), mostly health. The Peru version gives US$ 81,000-364,000 per kg (summary page).
- **Use:** turning hectares into money for FZS ("would FZS act on it"). Label as prediction/valuation, not a measured outcome.

## C. Enforcement, monitoring and displacement (causal designs)

### 14. Assunção, Gandour & Rocha (2023), AEJ: Applied
- **Citation:** Assunção J, Gandour C, Rocha R (2023) DETERring deforestation in the Amazon: environmental monitoring and law enforcement. *AEJ: Applied Economics* 15(2):125-156. https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20200196
- **Opened:** abstract (Semantic Scholar); fuller notes in `maap_citations.md`.
- **Key finding:** "Clouds limiting DETER's capacity to detect clearings serve as a source of exogenous variation for the presence of environmental authorities. Findings indicate that monitoring and enforcement effectively curb deforestation."
- **Use:** a model of a credible design (IV), to contrast with our single-unit DiD in part 4(c).

### 15. Saavedra (2021/2023), working paper + RCT
- **Citation:** Saavedra S (2021) The response of illegal mining to revealing its existence. SSRN WP. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3933128; AEA RCT Registry AEARCTR-0002397; IPA summary https://poverty-action.org/study/examining-response-revealing-existence-illegal-mining-colombia
- **Opened:** abstract (Semantic Scholar) + IPA summary page (full).
- **Key finding:** randomized disclosure of satellite-detected mines to local and/or national authorities in Colombia. "an eleven percent reduction in illegal mining in the exact mine locations and in the surrounding areas", but "illegal mining increased in areas not targeted by the information", so the net effect "decreased from an eleven percent reduction to a seven percent reduction".
- **Use:** direct experimental evidence of displacement. It shows a local effect overstates the regional effect, which is our "sum target + neighbours" check.

### 16. Saavedra & Romero (2021), European Economic Review
- **Citation:** Saavedra S, Romero M (2021) Local incentives and national tax evasion: the response of illegal mining to a tax reform in Colombia. *Eur. Econ. Rev.* 138:103843. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroecorev.2021.103843
- **Opened:** snippet (title/metadata only).
- **Key finding:** royalty-sharing reform → change in illegal mining (not read; do not quote).
- **Use:** possible reference for the tax/royalty channel (§4). Read before citing.

### 17. Börner, Kis-Katos, Hargrave & König (2015), PLoS ONE
- **Citation:** Börner J, Kis-Katos K, Hargrave J, König K (2015) Post-crackdown effectiveness of field-based forest law enforcement in the Brazilian Amazon. *PLoS ONE* 10(4):e0121544. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121544
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** ">15 thousand point coordinates of enforcement missions", 2009-10; field enforcement "not universally effective"; conservation effects of "4.0 and 9.9 hectares per inspection" in Mato Grosso and Pará; "highly cost-effective on average".
- **Use:** shows enforcement effects vary by place and agency. Supports "enforcement intensity", not a binary treatment.

### 18. Cisneros, Zhou & Börner (2015), PLoS ONE
- **Citation:** Cisneros E, Zhou SL, Börner J (2015) Naming and shaming for conservation: evidence from the Brazilian Amazon. *PLoS ONE* 10(9):e0136402. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136402
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** blacklisting municipalities (priority list) "considerably reduced deforestation"; matching + double differences, 2002-2012.
- **Use:** methods template (matched DiD with one policy cohort).

### 19. Nunes et al. (2024), Scientific Reports
- **Citation:** Nunes FSM, Soares-Filho BS, Oliveira AR, Veloso LVS, Schmitt J, van der Hoff R, et al. (2024) Lessons from the historical dynamics of environmental law enforcement in the Brazilian Amazon. *Sci. Rep.* 14:1828. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-52180-7
- **Opened:** abstract (also in `brazil_enforcement.md`).
- **Key finding:** "The number of embargoes and asset confiscations dropped by 59% and 55% in 2019 and 2020"; "militarization of enforcement between 2018 and 2022"; spending rose ("massive efficiency loss").
- **Use:** the citation for "Brazil enforcement weakened 2019-22" (§3b of agent.md).

### 20. Burgess, Hansen, Olken, Potapov & Sieber (2012), QJE
- **Citation:** Burgess R, Hansen M, Olken BA, Potapov P, Sieber S [listed as "Onder" in OpenAlex] (2012) The political economy of deforestation in the tropics. *QJE* 127(4):1707-1754. https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjs034
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** more jurisdictions → more illegal logging (Cournot competition); illegal logging and oil/gas rents are "short-run substitutes, but this effect disappears over time with political turnover".
- **Use:** theory that local officials' incentives drive illegal extraction. Background for the political-cycle hypothesis.

### 21. Pailler (2018), JEEM
- **Citation:** Pailler S (2018) Re-election incentives and deforestation cycles in the Brazilian Amazon. *J. Environ. Econ. Manage.* 88:345-365. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeem.2018.01.008
- **Opened:** snippet (no abstract available; result reported by Mongabay 2023 and Ruggiero et al. 2021).
- **Key finding (secondary):** municipal deforestation "+8-10%" in election years when the incumbent runs.
- **Use:** the only direct evidence for election cycles in Amazon land use. Read before citing.

### 22. Ruggiero, Pfaff, Nichols, Rosa & Metzger (2021), Conservation Letters
- **Citation:** Ruggiero PGC, Pfaff A, Nichols E, Rosa M, Metzger JP (2021) Election cycles affect deforestation within Brazil's Atlantic Forest. *Conserv. Lett.* 14:e12818. https://doi.org/10.1111/conl.12818
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** 2,253 municipalities, 1991-2014: higher-deforestation municipalities "see a significant rise in deforestation during federal-and-state election years"; stronger with party alignment.
- **Use:** election-cycle hypothesis (team).

### 23. Morpurgo et al. (2023), Biological Conservation
- **Citation:** Morpurgo J, Kissling WD, Tyrrell P, Negret PJ, van Bodegom PM, Allan JR (2023) *Biol. Conserv.* 279 (elections and deforestation in 55 tropical countries).
- **Opened:** snippet via Mongabay article (opened): "More deforestation occurs in years with competitive elections than in non-competitive election years". No Peru-specific result.
- **Use:** a caution that election effects are mixed.

### 24. Cisneros, Kis-Katos & Nuryartono (2021), JEEM
- **Citation:** Cisneros E, Kis-Katos K, Nuryartono N (2021) Palm oil and the politics of deforestation in Indonesia. *JEEM* 108:102453. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeem.2021.102453
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** "increases of about 4% in deforestation in the year prior to local mayoral elections"; up to 19% with high palm-price exposure.
- **Use:** the price × politics interaction. Same logic as gold price × weak enforcement in 2023 (agent.md confounder).

## D. Brazil: garimpo, Indigenous lands, gold trade

### 25. Siqueira-Gay & Sánchez (2021), Regional Environmental Change
- **Citation:** Siqueira-Gay J, Sánchez LE (2021) The outbreak of illegal gold mining in the Brazilian Amazon boosts deforestation. *Reg. Environ. Change* 21:28. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10113-021-01761-7
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** "illegal mining deforestation increased more than 90% from 2017 to 2020, reaching 101.7 km2 annually in 2020 compared to 52.9 km2 annually in 2017", driven by "increased prices and political pressure".
- **Use:** the Brazil weakening period (2019-22).

### 26. Cortinhas Ferreira Neto et al. (2024), Nature Communications
- **Citation:** Cortinhas Ferreira Neto L, Diniz CG, Maretto RV, Persello C, Pinheiro ML, Castro MC, et al. (2024) Uncontrolled illegal mining and garimpo in the Brazilian Amazon. *Nat. Commun.* 15. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54220-2
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** mechanized mining grew from 218 to 2,627 km² (1985-2022); "62% inside indigenous lands" are ≤5 years old; "At least 77% of the 2022 mechanized mining area shows explicit signs of illegality"; Kayapó, Munduruku and Yanomami hold ">90%" of TI garimpo.
- **Use:** justifies our choice of the three TIs; supports a legality proxy for Brazil.

### 27. Rodrigues (2025), Brazilian Political Science Review
- **Citation:** Rodrigues LA (2025) Brazil's journey against illegal mining. *BPSR* 19(2). https://doi.org/10.1590/1981-3821202500020006
- **Opened:** full (SciELO).
- **Key finding:** history of the gold rules: the good-faith presumption (Law 12,844/2013) suspended by the STF (Gilmar Mendes, ADI 7273); e-invoices mandatory from 1 Aug 2023; Escolhas: 282 t of gold with evidence of illegality 2015-2021.
- **Use:** §7 of the background file; the brief's Brazil framing.

### 28. Brasil (2026), Beijing Law Review
- **Citation:** Brasil DR (2026) Gold traceability, illegal mining, and ecological constitutionalism: regulatory accountability in ADI 7273/DF and 7345/DF. *Beijing Law Rev.* https://doi.org/10.4236/blr.2026.173040
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** a legal analysis of the joint STF decision on Art. 39 §4 of Law 12,844/2013.
- **Use:** citation for the two ADIs, if needed.

### 29. Castro et al. (2025), PLoS Neglected Tropical Diseases
- **Citation:** Castro MC, Arisco N, Diniz CG, Ponmattam J, Peterka C, Basta PC, Ferreira MU (2025) Mining and malaria in the Brazilian Amazon and in the Yanomami indigenous land. *PLoS NTD*. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0013677
- **Opened:** abstract.
- **Key finding:** "a 1% increase in the annual mining area was associated with a 24% [...] increase in monthly malaria cases in the Yanomami"; ~102,870 excess cases 2018-2023.
- **Use:** human cost of the Brazil case (talk, part 1 of the brief).

### 30. Santos, Moxotó & Dutra (2026), Sustainable Development
- **Citation:** Santos SL, Moxotó AC, Dutra JM (2026) Illegal gold mining in the Brazilian Amazon: environmental degradation in Yanomami Indigenous lands, and regulatory failures. *Sust. Dev.* https://doi.org/10.1002/sd.70821
- **Opened:** abstract (truncated).
- **Key finding:** 2008-2022 log-log OLS: Brazil's gold export value is strongly linked to Yanomami mined area.
- **Use:** gold price as confounder for Brazil; weak design (time series), cite with care.
