## 1. In one sentence

Compare how much the treated place changed with how much a comparison place changed over the same years; the difference of the two changes is the effect, if the two places would otherwise have moved in parallel.

## 2. Simple version

Two shops sell ice cream. In 2019 one shop (Tambopata) gets a new competitor across the street (Mercurio). Sales in that shop drop by 1,477 a year. But summer was also colder in 2019-21 (COVID, prices...). The other shop (Amarakaeri), which got no competitor, saw sales *rise* by 285 a year in the same years. So the best guess for "the competitor's effect" is

  (−1,477) − (+285) = −1,762.

The second shop tells us what the weather did. That only works if both shops would have moved together without the competitor (**parallel trends**), and if the competitor did not also steal customers *from* or *send* customers *to* the second shop (**no spillover**).

## 3. When to use it, and when not

**Use it when:**
- a policy hits some units and not others at a known date;
- you have data before and after, for both groups;
- the groups were moving in parallel before.

**Be careful or avoid it when:**
- the groups differ a lot in size or dynamics, because "parallel" then depends on the scale you choose (hectares vs %);
- the treatment can push the outcome into the comparison group (displacement);
- other shocks hit one group harder (COVID, a second operation);
- there is only one treated unit, because there is no sampling-based standard error.

## 4. Assumptions

| Assumption | In words | Formally |
|---|---|---|
| **Parallel trends** | Without treatment, the treated unit's outcome would have changed by the same amount as the comparison's | E[Y_T,post(0) − Y_T,pre(0)] = E[Y_C,post(0) − Y_C,pre(0)] |
| **No anticipation** | Nothing changed before the treatment date because of it | Y_T,pre(1) = Y_T,pre(0) |
| **No spillover (SUTVA)** | The treatment did not change the comparison's outcome | Y_C,post = Y_C,post(0) |
| **Stable composition** | The units are the same land before and after | fixed polygons |
| **Scale** | Parallel trends holds on the scale used (hectares here) | It cannot hold on both levels and logs unless the trends are flat |

## 5. The maths

### 5.1 Master's level

With group means Ȳ_g,p for group g ∈ {T, C} and period p ∈ {pre, post}:

  DiD = (Ȳ_T,post − Ȳ_T,pre) − (Ȳ_C,post − Ȳ_C,pre)

Equivalently, it is the coefficient β in the regression

  y_it = α + γ·T_i + δ·Post_t + β·(T_i × Post_t) + ε_it.

**Decomposition.**
  DiD = τ_T + [parallel-trends gap] − [spillover into C]

- Under parallel trends and no spillover, DiD = τ_T, the effect on the treated.
- If the treated unit drives some outcome into C (s_C > 0) while its own outcome falls (τ_T < 0), the DiD is more negative than τ_T. That bias is **away from zero**.

### 5.2 PhD level

**Scale dependence of parallel trends** (Roth and Sant'Anna 2023). Parallel trends in levels and in logs can both hold only in degenerate cases. With units of very different size, the choice is a substantive assumption:
- Levels (ha) assume the counterfactual change in Tambopata equals Amarakaeri's change in hectares.
- Proportional (log or % of stock) assumes equal growth *rates*.

Here the two give the same sign but very different magnitudes. Report both.

**Inference with one treated unit.** With N_T = N_C = 1 there is no sampling distribution for β. Under a sharp null (τ = 0 in every year), a **placebo in time** compares the real DiD to DiDs computed at fake start dates, using the same window lengths. The rank of the real value is a randomization p-value if the fake dates are exchangeable with the real one. Overlapping windows break that, so fakes must end before 2019.

**Bounding spillover.** If spillover s_C ≥ 0 is unknown, the true effect lies between:
- the DiD, assuming C's whole change is its own trend; and
- Tambopata's own before/after change (assuming C's whole change is spillover and the counterfactual trend is 0).

Both ends rely on assumptions, so this is a sensitivity range, not an identified set (Manski-style bounds would need a monotonicity assumption on the counterfactual trend).

## 7. Tests run and results

### T1 · Reproduce

Tambopata 1,640 → 163, Amarakaeri 289 → 574 ha/yr, DiD **−1,761.8** ha/yr. This matches `did_main` and the course card exactly. Using Madre de Dios rows only gives −1,731 (the numbers in agent.md §10).

### T2 · Choice of pre-window (post fixed at 2019-21)

| Pre-window | Tambopata change | Amarakaeri change | DiD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016-18 (main) | −1,477 | +285 | **−1,762** |
| 2014-18 | −1,432 | +232 | −1,663 |
| 2011-18 | −1,327 | +123 | −1,450 |
| 2016-17 | −1,418 | +167 | −1,586 |
| 2015-17 | −1,379 | +202 | −1,581 |
| 2017-18 | −1,943 | +321 | −2,264 |
| 2014-16 | −1,091 | +172 | −1,263 |

**The sign never changes. The size ranges from −1,263 to −2,264 depending on the window.** Two single years drive the spread: Tambopata's 2016 dip (707) and Amarakaeri's 2018 low (54). The main window contains both. A longer baseline (2011-18 or 2014-18) gives −1,450 to −1,663.

### T3 · Placebo in time: every fake start year

Same 3-year windows, fake start *s*, pre = s−3…s−1, post = s…s+2. "Clean" means the post window ends by 2018.

| Fake start | 2003 | 2004 | 2005 | 2006 | 2007 | 2008 | 2009 | 2010 | 2011 | 2012 | 2013 | 2014 | 2015 | 2016 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DiD | −120 | −336 | −587 | −446 | −280 | +250 | +449 | +814 | +1,034 | +905 | +927 | +167 | +364 | +209 |

- Real −1,762 ranks **1 of 15** (one-sided p = 0.07, also rank 1 two-sided). It is 3.2 standard deviations of the fakes from zero.
- But the fakes are **not centred on zero**. From 2008 to 2016 every fake is positive (mean of all clean fakes +239). Tambopata was growing faster than Amarakaeri for a decade.
- The course's single placebo (2016: +209) is one of the smaller ones.

What this means: parallel trends in hectares already failed before 2019, in the direction of Tambopata rising faster. If that divergence had continued, the counterfactual DiD would be positive and the true effect larger than −1,762. If it was ending (2014-16 fakes are smaller; in log terms Tambopata's growth was already slowing, S10 T6), the effect is about as estimated or smaller. So the direction of this bias depends on the scale, and it is listed that way in S14 §8. Either way, the placebo pattern is not consistent with "no effect".

### T4 · Scale

| Scale | Tambopata pre → post | Amarakaeri pre → post | DiD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hectares (main) | 1,640 → 163 | 289 → 574 | −1,762 ha/yr |
| % of last year's stock | 17.6% → 1.3% | 4.2% → 7.1% | **−19.3 pts** |
| Relative to own 2016-18 mean | 1 → 0.10 | 1 → 1.98 | −1.89 |
| log(1 + addition) | | | −4.47 (unreliable: Tambopata's −111 in 2021 is clipped to 0) |

The sign is robust to scale. In growth-rate terms Tambopata went from growing about four times faster than Amarakaeri to five times slower.

### T5 · What the DiD is made of: new, re-mined, stopped

Raster zones: Tambopata = La Pampa sector + rest of the Tambopata BZ; Amarakaeri = raster zone 9.

| Component | Tambopata change | Amarakaeri change | DiD |
|---|---|---|---|
| New land (first-time) | −723 | +10 | **−733** |
| Re-mined | +141 | +1 | +140 |
| Stopped | +605 | −2 | **+607** (enters with −) |
| Net | −1,186 | +13 | −1,199 |

Of the raster net DiD (−1,199), about 61% is less first-time mining and about 51% is more land stopping, with re-mining offsetting 12%.

Warning: the raster "Amarakaeri BZ" zone holds almost no mining (47 ha in 2018 vs 7,512 ha in the workbook). See T6b.

### T6 · Spillover and the comparison unit

**T6a · Spillover bound**
- If all of Amarakaeri's +285 is spillover from Tambopata, the effect on Tambopata is at most its own change, **−1,477**.
- If none of it is spillover, it is −1,762.
- The 13 other zones together changed by only +65 ha/yr, so they cannot absorb displaced miners at this scale.

**T6b · New finding: what "Amarakaeri buffer zone" actually is.**
I intersected the SERNANP Amarakaeri buffer-zone polygon with our raster zone map and the first-mined year (`zones_v2_mdd_full.tif`).

| Land ever mined by 2018 inside the BZ polygon | Amarakaeri BZ | Tambopata BZ |
|---|---|---|
| In the **legal mining corridor** (D.L. 1100) | **5,937 ha (58%)** | 18 ha (0.1%) |
| In **native communities** | **4,220 ha (41%)** | 2,639 ha (17%, Kotsimba) |
| In the BZ outside corridor and communities | 82 ha (1%) | 13,315 ha (83%; La Pampa sector 11,355) |

**So the 2×2 compares mostly illegal buffer-zone mining (Tambopata) with mostly legal-corridor and community mining (Amarakaeri).** Consequences:
1. The comparison is not "another illegal front without Mercurio". It is the legal corridor around Huepetuhe, whose rules, enforcement and incentives differ.
2. Displacement from La Pampa into the corridor (S8, S9) would raise the comparison. This is exactly the spillover that biases the DiD away from zero.
3. This also explains why Amarakaeri behaves like the "rest of the corridor" (rising through 2019-25).

### T7 · Persistence (later years)

| Later-years window | Tambopata change | Amarakaeri change | DiD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-25 (main) | +190 | +927 | −737 |
| 2022, 2024, 2025 (no 2023) | −389 | +632 | −1,021 |
| 2022 only | −1,059 | +797 | −1,857 |
| 2024-25 | −54 | +550 | −604 |

**The negative persistence DiD comes from the comparison rising, not from Tambopata staying low.** By 2022-25 Tambopata is back at, or slightly above, its 2016-18 level (+190). So "persistence" should be described as: "Tambopata returned to its earlier pace; the comparison kept growing".

## 8. Assumption check

| Assumption | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel trends (ha) | **Not supported before 2019** | Fakes 2008-16 all positive, mean +239 (T3) |
| Parallel trends (%) | Not tested over time; same sign (T4) | none |
| No anticipation | Unclear | 2017-18 operations sit in the pre-window; Tambopata 2018 is high, Amarakaeri 2018 is a low outlier |
| No spillover | **Likely violated** | Amarakaeri BZ mining is 58% legal corridor (T6b), the corridor rose (S1 T1), Camanti displacement (MAAP #130) |
| Comparison untreated | **Violated** | Mercurio II covered all of Madre de Dios; 2023 emergency includes Huepetuhe |
| Stable composition | Holds | Fixed polygons; workbook areas constant |

## 9. Weaknesses, ranked

1. **The comparison is a different kind of place.** Amarakaeri BZ mining is 99% corridor and community land. The DiD contrasts illegal BZ mining with largely legal corridor mining, which is also where displaced miners would go.
2. **Window sensitivity.** −1,263 to −2,264. The main window contains both influential single years (Tambopata 2016, Amarakaeri 2018).
3. **Pre-trends in hectares were not parallel.** Tambopata outgrew Amarakaeri for a decade (fakes 2008-16 positive).
4. **About half the net DiD is abandonment** (more land leaving the mining class), not less new clearing (T5).
5. **"Persistence" is mislabelled.** Tambopata itself recovered; the negative DiD in 2022-25 is the comparison's growth.
6. **Wording.** Make sure the brief does not repeat the course card's "Amarakaeri, not targeted".

## 11. Verdict

The 2×2 is computed correctly and its **sign is robust** to window, scale and spillover bounds. Every variant gives a large relative fall for Tambopata in 2019-21. Its **size is not pinned down**: −1,263 to −2,264 ha/yr by window, and −1,477 to −1,762 ha/yr by spillover assumption.

Its **comparison is weak**:
- it is mostly legal-corridor land;
- it is partly treated;
- it is the likely destination of displaced miners;
- it was not on a parallel path before 2019.

Label: **description**. Wording for the brief: "Tambopata's net additions fell from 1,640 to 163 ha/yr in 2019-21 while Amarakaeri's rose from 289 to 574. The difference (−1,762 ha/yr) is the largest of any three-year window since 2003 [D]."
